Logging In & Access Control

What This Does

The login process confirms who you are and decides which parts of the system you can access. Every user belongs to a role, and each role has its own set of permissions.

Super (Admin)
  • Full access to everything
  • Can manage users, zones, racks
  • Can see all staff's records
Staff / Checker
  • Create pickup requests
  • Record lost & found items
  • View own records only
Picker
  • Accept and start picking tasks
  • Complete putaway into racks
  • Complete stock movements

What Happens Step by Step

  • 1
    You open the application
    The app immediately checks if you were already logged in before. If yes, it loads your last saved data from the device and takes you straight to the dashboard — instantly, with no loading wait. This works even if the internet is temporarily unavailable.
  • 2
    You enter your name and password
    Your credentials are compared against the user list stored in the system (which comes from the Google Sheets staff database). The check is done entirely on the device — no slow server round-trip needed for login itself.
  • 3
    Login succeeds — your session is saved
    A secure session record is saved on your device with a 4-hour expiry timer. The app then downloads the latest product catalogue, zone list, rack list, checker lines, and user list from Google Sheets and stores them on your device for fast access throughout your shift.
  • 4
    Session automatically expires after 4 hours
    Every 15 seconds the app quietly checks your session timer in the background. When it expires, a lock screen appears prompting you to log in again. Your unsaved work is not lost — you simply re-authenticate to continue.
i
What is saved: Your login session (name, role, expiry time) is saved on your browser's local storage. It does not travel to any server during logout — it is simply deleted from the device.

Creating a Pickup Request

What This Does

A Pickup Request is a formal request to retrieve a specific product from a checker line. When a checker or staff member needs an item picked up and moved into stock, they create a request here. The system assigns a unique tracking ID and routes the request to the picking queue.

!
Who can do this: Staff and Checker roles. Pickers and Admins can also see these requests but they are typically created by the person at the checker line.

What Happens Step by Step

  • 1
    You click "New Pickup Request"
    The form opens. The system immediately generates a unique ticket number for this request — for example RC-482917. This number is calculated locally on your device, so it appears instantly without any waiting.
  • 2
    You select the SKU code (product code)
    When you pick a product code from the searchable list, the system automatically fills in the product name, category, and whether it is Food or Non-Food — based on the product catalogue that was downloaded when you logged in. Nothing is fetched from the internet at this moment.
  • 3
    You select the Checker Line and enter the quantity
    The checker line list comes from your locally stored reference data. You also enter how many units are needed and any relevant notes about this request.
  • 4
    You submit the request
    The system immediately adds this request to your device's local list so you can see it right away. At the same time, the request is sent to Google Sheets in the background. The status is set to Pending — meaning it is waiting to be picked. If you are a non-admin, you only see requests created under your name.
+
Behind the scenes: After the form is submitted, 2.5 seconds later the system re-downloads the latest request list from Google Sheets to make sure your local data reflects any changes made by other team members.

Picking Items from Checker Lines

What This Does

The Picking Task section shows all open pickup requests and lost & found items that are waiting to be physically collected from checker lines. A picker selects which items they are going to pick up, confirms with a swipe gesture, and the tasks are officially assigned to them.

What Happens Step by Step

  • 1
    You open the Picking Task section
    The app immediately fetches the latest pending requests from Google Sheets. The Waiting List shows all items with status Pending from both the Pickup Request list and the Lost & Found list. A badge on the tab tells you how many are waiting.
  • 2
    You select which items you will pick
    On desktop, tick the checkboxes. On mobile, tap each task card to highlight it. A floating bar at the bottom shows how many items you have selected, for example "Start Picking (4)".
  • 3
    You confirm by swiping right
    When you tap "Start Picking", a confirmation panel appears showing the list of items you selected. You drag a slider all the way to the right (at least 90% of the way) to confirm. This extra step prevents accidental starts. Once confirmed, all selected items are instantly assigned to you.
  • 4
    Tasks move to "Picking — In Progress"
    Each task gets a unique Picking ID (e.g. PK-391042), your name is recorded as the picker, and the status changes from Pending to Picking. The original requests (Pickup Request or Lost & Found) are also updated to reflect this status. Everything is saved both locally and in Google Sheets.
i
Visibility rules: Super and Manager roles can see all picking tasks from all staff. Regular pickers only see tasks assigned to themselves.

Putting Items Away into Racks (Putaway)

What This Does

After physically picking up items from the checker line, the picker places them into a warehouse rack location. This is called Putaway. When you record a putaway, the system automatically updates the live stock count (SOH — Stock on Hand) for that product at that rack location.

What Happens Step by Step

  • 1
    You click "Complete / Putaway" on a task
    In the Picking In Progress tab, find your task and click the complete button. A form opens pre-filled with the product details.
  • 2
    You select the destination rack
    A searchable location picker appears showing all racks in the warehouse, filtered by zone. You select the exact rack where you are placing the items — for example A-01-02.
  • 3
    You confirm the quantity and submit
    The quantity defaults to the full task amount. You can adjust it if you are doing a partial putaway. When you submit, several things happen at once automatically — see below.
  • 4
    The system updates all related records
    The putaway record is saved. A stock activity log entry is created (showing stock moved from the checker line to the rack). The current stock count for that product at that rack location is updated. If the full quantity has been put away, the task is marked Completed.
+
How the stock count is updated: The system checks whether this product already exists at this rack location in the stock list. If yes, it adds your quantity to the existing count. If no, it creates a new stock record for this product-location combination. This update goes directly to Google Sheets, adjusting the SOH number in real time.

Viewing Current Stock — Stock on Hand (SOH)

What This Does

The Stock on Hand (SOH) screen shows you the live inventory count for every product at every rack location across the warehouse. Numbers here are automatically updated whenever a putaway is completed or a stock movement/deduction is processed.

What You Can See

  • Total SKUs in stock — how many unique products are currently stored
  • Total quantity across all racks — the warehouse-wide item count
  • High-age stock — products that have been sitting for a long time without movement
  • Deduction required — items flagged as damaged, expired, or missing

You can filter the list by product code, product name, category, rack location, or how long the stock has been stored.

i
Where the numbers come from: Every time a putaway is completed, the SOH count at that rack increases. Every time a stock movement or deduction is completed, the SOH count decreases. The SOH screen always reflects the current state of the Google Sheets database, fetched fresh when you navigate to this section.

Removing Damaged or Lost Stock — Stock Deduction

What This Does

When stock needs to be removed from the warehouse — because it is damaged, expired, missing, or there is a count discrepancy — you create a Stock Deduction. This creates a task in the system that must be reviewed and completed before the stock count is actually reduced.

What Happens Step by Step

  • 1
    You find the product in the SOH list
    Navigate to the Stock on Hand section, find the specific product-rack row, and click "Deduct Stock".
  • 2
    You fill in the deduction form
    The product details and current rack location are already filled in. You choose a reason for the deduction: Bad/Damaged/Expired, Lost/Missing, Discrepancy Correction, or Sample/Testing. The "destination" is automatically set to Deduction and cannot be changed — this signals that the stock is leaving the warehouse entirely.
  • 3
    A task is created in the movement queue
    The system does not immediately reduce the stock count. Instead, it creates a Stock Movement task with type "Stock Deduction" and status "Assigned". This task must then be verified and completed (see Section 7) before the stock number actually changes.
!
Why the two-step process? This prevents accidental or fraudulent stock removals. A second staff member must physically verify the SKU code before the count is reduced in the system.

Moving Stock Between Locations

What This Does

The Stock Movement section handles two types of tasks: physically transferring stock between rack locations (e.g., moving items from rack A-01 to rack B-03), and completing stock deductions that were created in the SOH section. Both require a double-verification step to prevent mistakes.

The Double-Verification Process

When you click "Complete Task" on a movement, the system walks you through two verification steps before anything is changed:

  • 1
    Step 1 — Verify the SKU Code
    You scan or manually type the product code (SKU) of the item you are holding. The system checks it against the expected SKU for this task. If they do not match, the process is blocked and an error is shown. You must scan the correct item to continue.
  • 2
    Step 2 — Verify the Location
    For a transfer, you confirm the destination rack where you are placing the items. For a deduction, the location is automatically "Deduction" and does not need to be entered manually.
  • 3
    Click "Verify & Complete"
    Once both steps pass, the system executes all changes simultaneously: the movement is marked Done, the stock count at the source location decreases, and (for transfers only) the stock count at the destination location increases. Two activity log entries are created.
+
For transfers: Two log entries are created — one showing stock going out (minus) from the source rack, and one showing stock coming in (plus) to the destination rack. This gives a complete audit trail for every movement.

Recording Found Items — Lost & Found

What This Does

When a staff member finds a product that does not have a home — it is sitting somewhere in the warehouse without being properly recorded in stock — they log it in the Lost & Found section. This registers the item and automatically puts it into the picking waiting list so it can be properly put away into a rack and added to the official stock count.

What Happens Step by Step

  • 1
    You click "New Lost & Found Entry"
    A form opens with a pre-generated ticket ID — for example LF-728341. This ID is created instantly on your device.
  • 2
    You fill in the item details
    Enter the product code (SKU), product name, and quantity found. Then select the zone and specific location where you found the item using the location picker. Add any notes about why it was found there.
  • 3
    You submit the entry
    The entry is saved locally and sent to Google Sheets. Status is set to Pending. The item immediately appears in the Picking Task Waiting List — so any picker can pick it up and put it away into a proper rack location, at which point it will count as official inventory in the SOH.

Managing Users, Zones & Racks (Admin Only)

What This Does

The Admin Panel is accessible only to users with the Super role. It is used to set up and maintain all the reference data the rest of the system depends on — who can log in, which warehouse zones exist, which rack locations exist, and which checker lines are active.

!
Important: Changes made in the Admin Panel affect everyone using the system. Adding or removing a rack location, for example, immediately updates the list of available locations in the Putaway and Stock Movement pickers for all staff.

What You Can Manage

SectionWhat You Can DoWhat Gets Updated
User Management Add new staff accounts, change roles and passwords, remove accounts Google Sheets User_DB sheet; local device cache updated immediately
Zone Management Add, rename, or delete warehouse zones Google Sheets Zone sheet; affects zone picker in forms
Rack Management Add new rack locations with zone, aisle, bay, and capacity details; rename or delete racks Google Sheets Racks sheet; affects location pickers in Putaway and Stock Movement
Checker Line Management Add, rename, or delete checker line identifiers Google Sheets Checker_Lines sheet; affects checker line picker in Pickup Requests

How Changes Are Saved

Every add, edit, or delete in the Admin Panel follows the same reliable two-step process:

  1. The change is applied instantly on your device — you see it immediately in the list.
  2. The change is sent to Google Sheets in the background. After 2.5 seconds, the system re-downloads that list from Google Sheets to confirm the change was saved successfully.

How Your Data Is Saved & Kept Fresh

The Big Picture

IRMS is designed to feel fast and work reliably even with a slow or unstable internet connection. It achieves this by keeping a complete copy of all your data on the device and syncing with Google Sheets in the background. You never wait for the internet to use the app.

Two Layers of Storage

Storage LayerWhat It StoresHow Fast
Device Database (IndexedDB) All operational data: requests, tasks, stock, movements, lost & found. Also the reference data: products, racks, zones, users. Instant — 0ms loading. Available even without internet.
Google Sheets (Cloud) The permanent, authoritative record of all data. What Google Sheets holds is always the "truth". Fetched in background. Requires internet but does not block your work.

When Data Is Fetched from Google Sheets

Data TypeWhen It Is DownloadedWhy Not More Often
Product catalogue, Racks, Zones, Users Once per login session or when you press F5 (refresh) These change rarely. Downloading them every navigation wastes time and bandwidth.
Pickup Requests Every time you navigate to the Pickup Request section Changes frequently — new requests are created throughout the shift
Picking Tasks, SOH, Stock Movement Every time you navigate to those sections Changes frequently as work progresses
Lost & Found Every time you navigate to the Lost & Found section Changes throughout the shift

Background Auto-Refresh

Every 60 seconds, the app quietly checks whether the data for the section you are currently viewing has become stale (older than 1 minute). If it has, the app automatically re-downloads fresh data in the background without interrupting your work.

Manual Refresh Options

You can also manually refresh at any time using the sync button in the header. You have two options:

  • Regular Refresh — Downloads fresh data only for the section you are currently on. Fast and efficient.
  • Flush Cache & Full Resync — Completely clears all locally stored data and re-downloads everything from Google Sheets from scratch. Use this if something seems out of sync.

What Happens If You Lose Internet?

+
If your internet connection drops, the app continues working using your locally stored data. You can still view all your records. However, new entries you create will not reach Google Sheets until the connection is restored. Once internet returns, any pending sync requests are completed automatically.
